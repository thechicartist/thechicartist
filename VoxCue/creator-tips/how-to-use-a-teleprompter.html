How to Use a Teleprompter Without Sounding Like You're Reading

A teleprompter should help you communicate naturally—not make you sound like you're reciting a script. Here's how to use one while staying confident, conversational and connected to your audience.

7 MIN READ · VOXCUE

A teleprompter is supposed to make recording easier.

But if you've ever watched yourself back and thought, "Why do I sound like I'm reading?" you're not alone.

The goal of a teleprompter isn't to make you read your script perfectly. It's to help you remember what you want to say while still allowing you to talk naturally to the camera.

Here are some simple ways to make your next teleprompter video sound more natural.

1. Don't write your script like an essay

This is one of the biggest mistakes people make.

When you write something for reading, you naturally use longer sentences and more formal language.

But that's not how we normally talk.

Instead of:

"Today, I would like to discuss three important strategies that can help you improve the quality of your videos."

Try:

"Today, I want to share three simple ways to make your videos better."

Write your script the way you actually speak.

A good test is to read your script out loud before recording. If a sentence feels awkward when you say it, rewrite it.

2. Don't try to memorize every word

A teleprompter should take the pressure off you.

You don't need to memorize your entire script and you don't need to deliver every sentence perfectly.

Focus on the idea you're communicating.

Think of your script as a guide rather than something you have to recite word-for-word.

That small change can make you sound much more relaxed.

3. Look at the camera, not at your script

This is where a teleprompter can make a huge difference.

When your script is positioned close to the camera lens, you can read while maintaining eye contact with your audience.

That makes your video feel much more like a conversation.

And eye contact matters.

Even though you're speaking to a camera, the person watching should feel like you're speaking directly to them.

4. Let the script move at your pace

If the text is scrolling too quickly, you'll rush.

If it's too slow, you'll start waiting for the next line.

Neither feels natural.

Your teleprompter should adapt to the way you speak.

This is one of the reasons voice-activated scrolling can be so useful.

5. Use pauses

Don't be afraid of silence.

A short pause can make your video feel more natural and give your audience a moment to process what you've said.

If you're rushing through your script because you're trying to keep up with the text, slow down.

Breathe.

Pause.

Then continue.

Your audience isn't in a hurry.

6. Talk to one person

Instead of imagining you're speaking to hundreds or thousands of people, imagine you're talking to one person.

This simple trick can completely change your delivery.

Imagine you're explaining something to a friend.

Your voice will naturally become warmer, your expressions will become more natural, and your video will feel less rehearsed.

7. Don't aim for a perfect first take

Even experienced creators don't always get the perfect take on the first attempt.

If you stumble over a sentence, pause and continue.

You can always edit later.

The important thing is to keep your attention on your message instead of worrying about every tiny mistake.

The best teleprompter is the one you don't have to think about

A teleprompter should disappear into the background.

You shouldn't be thinking:

  • "I need to scroll now."
  • "I'm getting ahead of the text."
  • "Where am I in the script?"

You should be thinking about your audience and what you want to say.
