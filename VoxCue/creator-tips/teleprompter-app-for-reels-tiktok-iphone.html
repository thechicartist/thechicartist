Short video is unforgiving. You have about three seconds to sound confident — and no time to memorize, stumble, or start over five times.

A Reel or TikTok is usually under a minute long, which makes it tempting to just wing it. But winging it is why so many short videos ramble, repeat the same point twice, or end without ever saying the thing they were supposed to say. A short script fixes that — if you can read it without looking like you are reading.

That is what a teleprompter app is for. On an iPhone, it turns your phone into both the camera and the script, so you can record vertical video hands-free and sound like yourself while you do it.

Why short video is harder than it looks

Long videos forgive a slow start. Short videos do not. If the first line is vague, viewers swipe. If you pause to remember what comes next, viewers swipe. If you sound like you are reading a paragraph, viewers swipe — because reading looks and sounds different from talking, and people can feel the difference in a second.

At the same time, the best short videos are tightly structured. There is a hook in the first line, one clear idea in the middle, and a simple ending. That structure almost always comes from a script, even a short one. The creators who look effortless usually wrote down exactly what they wanted to say first.

The trick is keeping the script invisible. The words should be close to the lens, the sentences should sound spoken, and the pace should be yours — not the pace of a scrolling speed you guessed before you started.

How a teleprompter works for vertical video

Record in portrait mode with the front camera, and place the script near the top of the screen, close to the lens. When you read, your eyes stay near the camera, so you keep eye contact with the viewer. That is the whole secret: eye contact makes a video feel like a conversation, and a conversation holds attention.

Write a 30-second script that works

A Reel or TikTok script is not a mini essay. It is usually three parts, and it fits on a phone screen.

  1. The hook (one line). Say the most interesting thing first. “The mistake that ruins your watercolor” beats “Hi everyone, welcome back to my channel” every time.
  2. The point (two or three short lines). One idea, stated plainly. If you need a second idea, that is a second video.
  3. The ending (one line). Tell viewers what to do next, or leave them with one thing to remember. Then stop.

Write it the way you talk. Read it out loud once before you record, and cut any sentence you stumble over. Short sentences, contractions, and line breaks where you want to breathe will do more for your delivery than any camera setting.

If a sentence feels awkward to say out loud, it will feel awkward to watch. Rewrite it until it sounds like you.

Five practical tips for Reels and TikTok

1. Record at eye level

Put the phone at eye level, not below your chin. A low angle is unflattering and makes reading the script harder, because your eyes have further to travel.

2. Keep the light in front of you

Face a window or a lamp. Light from behind turns you into a silhouette, and no teleprompter fixes that.

3. Do a 10-second test first

Record ten seconds, watch it back, and check two things: can you read the script comfortably, and do your eyes look at the camera? Adjust text size or distance before the real take.

4. Record in sections

You do not need one perfect take. Record the hook, pause, record the middle, pause, record the ending. Short sections are easier to deliver well and easy to trim together.

5. Stop when the point is made

Short video rewards brevity. If you have said the point, end the video. Viewers remember a video that ended well; they swipe away from one that kept going.

What about sounding natural?

The fear most people have about teleprompters is sounding robotic. That usually comes from the script, not the tool. A script written like an essay will sound like an essay, however you read it. A script written like a conversation — short sentences, your own words, pauses where you would naturally pause — sounds like a conversation.
